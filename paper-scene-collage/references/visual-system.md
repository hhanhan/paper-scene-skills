# Visual System

## Design Aim

Build a flat editorial artifact in which photography, paper, and print processes form one spatial system. The source must remain recognizably photographic in a protected core, yet the page must not read as a rectangular photo pasted onto textured stock. Every added mark should emerge from the source's geometry, material, light, repetition, or movement.

## Page Topologies

Choose one primary topology after reading the source morphology.

### Converted Field

Keep the nearest or most important subject photographic while translating a surrounding depth band into linework, wash, screen tone, or paper color. Let the transition follow spatial depth or a source contour rather than a neat frame.

### Interlocking Islands

Use one dominant photographic core and a small number of related source islands. Connect them with source-derived shapes or marks so they read as one event, not a contact sheet.

### Expanding Edge

Let an irregular photographic region dissolve outward into an illustrated continuation. Extend trees, reflections, clouds, architecture, fabric, shadow, or motion using a simplified process that preserves their direction and rhythm.

### Layered Crossing

Organize the page around a strong horizontal, diagonal, or curved source structure. Allow photographic material, printed fields, and paper cut forms to pass in front of and behind one another while staying visually flat.

### Atmospheric Basin

Keep a dense central or lower photographic region and let weather, water, sky, haze, or ground continue as a broad printed field. Use quiet paper at the perimeter, but maintain a visible route back into the source.

## Interlock Plan

The selected topology must include all of the following:

- a protected photographic core large enough to carry the subject or relationship;
- at least one source-derived continuation that crosses or dissolves a boundary;
- a middle-scale counterform that balances the focal core;
- an intentional detail gradient from photographic specificity through print simplification to quiet stock;
- a clear layer order, such as stock, printed atmosphere, photo core, overprint, and optional type.

Avoid four uniformly treated edges. A clean rectangular aperture is acceptable only when another substantial layer breaks, crosses, or converts it; a thin border, tape strip, or decorative cloud does not count.

## Shape Harvesting

Derive added forms from visible source morphology:

- turn skyline, branch, shoreline, roof, garment, shadow, or gesture contours into broad paper cuts;
- turn repeated windows, people, trees, snow, ripples, or masonry into controlled print rhythms;
- turn a depth band into a wash or screen field rather than tracing every detail;
- reuse a minor source shape at a different scale to create an echo;
- preserve asymmetry and irregular intervals when they carry the scene's character.

Do not add generic stickers, arrows, clouds, circles, or abstract squiggles unless the same behavior is visibly supported by the source.

## Material Orchestra

Choose one dominant production process and one subordinate process. Their jobs must differ.

- **tonal screenprint** for broad converted fields and spatial depth;
- **dry ink or graphite** for structural contours and fine echoes;
- **opaque cut paper** for counterforms and clear overlaps;
- **torn fiber** for selected transitions where erosion, weather, or softness is relevant;
- **dilute wash** for atmosphere, reflection, haze, or snow;
- **localized halftone** for compression, not as a full-page filter.

Keep the page physically flat: no cast shadows, curled corners, raised stickers, product mockup, or theatrical paper sculpture.

## Color Architecture

Build a small hierarchy rather than defaulting to one isolated accent:

- a stock tone;
- one dominant ink family that organizes the converted field;
- an optional muted companion tone that separates depth or material;
- an optional compact signal color tied to a focal relation or directional transition.

Every color must have a structural job. Pull the system from source color temperature or a justified counterpoint. Avoid fashionable color for its own sake and avoid letting full-color photography fight an unrelated print palette.

## Density Rhythm

Design at three viewing scales:

- thumbnail: one legible focal relationship and directional path;
- normal scale: clear middle-scale paper or print structures that interlock with the photo;
- close view: selective grain, fibers, broken ink, or registration evidence.

Quiet space is active only when it counterbalances density or carries direction. Do not leave a large blank field simply because the style is described as restrained.

## Optional Typography

Use only user-supplied words. Treat type as another printed layer with a clear alignment to the page topology. Prefer small editorial, typewriter, pencil, or restrained print voices. Never manufacture titles, documentary metadata, quotations, credits, signatures, or watermarks.
