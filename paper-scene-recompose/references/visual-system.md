# Visual System

## Design Aim

Create an independent editorial illustration that is materially rich, semantically specific, and unmistakably non-photographic. Preserve the source relationship through posture, interval, direction, color partition, environmental pressure, and motif echoes. Restraint means controlled hierarchy, not an automatic formula of one silhouette, one line, and empty paper.

## Illustration Score

Build the selected direction from four related roles:

- **focal assembly**: the main subject or relationship, expressed through several interlocking shapes rather than one undifferentiated silhouette;
- **relational echo**: a smaller form, gap, trace, or counterpart that makes the action or interval readable;
- **environmental field**: source-grounded weather, foliage, architecture, water, ground, light, or temporal pressure translated into paper or print behavior;
- **counterform**: a mass or opening that redirects the eye and prevents the focal assembly from floating.

The roles need not have equal visual weight, but each must be visually locatable. They may share a boundary or material, yet the focal assembly cannot stand in for its own relational echo, an unshaped blank area cannot count as an environmental field or counterform, and one connector cannot carry the entire page. Omit a role only under an explicit user override that is recorded in the direction fingerprint and still preserves the source relationship.

## Composition Engines

Choose one primary engine.

### Directional Relay

Use a gesture, gaze, path, or directional force to pass attention between a focal assembly and a smaller echo. Let the connector change density, scale, or material along its path so it feels enacted rather than drawn as a generic smooth arc.

### Shelter And Exposure

Build a protected inner form against an enclosing or opening field. Express pressure through overlap, interrupted edges, compressed intervals, and a release toward active paper space.

### Strata And Passage

Translate ground, snow, water, architecture, or time into layered bands crossed by the subject. Vary edge character and translucency so the layers describe movement and depth without becoming scenery illustration.

### Scatter And Gather

Break a source rhythm into fragments that disperse, collect, or change state around the focal assembly. Preserve a readable origin and destination; avoid decorative confetti.

### Paired Weight

Use two differently scaled masses or clusters whose distance, tilt, overlap, or interruption carries the central relationship. Add an environmental trace so the pair does not float on blank stock.

## Motif Construction

Build motifs from the sealed source reading:

- retain posture through joint direction and body mass partition rather than facial likeness;
- translate a distinctive object or counterpart into a simplified but recognizable shape family;
- convert branches, rails, shadows, drifts, ripples, or paths into framing or directional structures;
- repeat one source-derived shape at contrasting scales to create memory or temporal echo;
- use absence as a shaped interval, not an unbounded empty background.

Reject a motif when it would fit almost any source unchanged. Generic suns, moons, birds, doors, circles, mountains, and ribbons require direct support from the source relationship or the user's brief.

## Material Orchestra

Choose one dominant process and one subordinate process with separate jobs:

- cut or torn paper for body partitions, enclosures, and broad counterforms;
- dry ink, graphite, or charcoal for branches, gesture traces, and broken contours;
- screenprint or stencil for atmospheric fields and repeated rhythm;
- translucent wash for weather, light, water, or temporal drift;
- localized halftone or grain for recession and disintegration.

Vary edge character deliberately between crisp cut, exposed fiber, dry breakup, overprint, and quiet stock. Do not apply the same distressed edge to every form.

## Color Architecture

Use a compact system with distinct roles:

- paper or stock tone;
- one dominant ink or paper family for the main structural field;
- one or two muted companion tones to separate body parts, depth, weather, or counterpart;
- an optional compact signal color for the decisive gesture, interval, or transition.

Color partition can preserve source specificity even when identity is generalized. Avoid collapsing a multicolored subject into a single black mass unless the source reading and selected direction genuinely require it.

## Density And Active Quiet

Distribute information across scales:

- a clear focal relationship at thumbnail size;
- middle-scale layers, echoes, or counterforms at normal size;
- selective fibers, broken ink, registration, or grain at close view.

Quiet space must be shaped by nearby direction, interval, or material fade. A broad blank field with no tension is unfinished, not refined. Conversely, do not fill every area; concentrate detail where it carries meaning.

## Identity Abstraction

Generalize faces and incidental identifiers while retaining semantically necessary posture, body partition, gesture, clothing-color relation, or age category only when directly visible and needed. Do not invent ethnicity, health, occupation, biography, or identity. For children or other sensitive subjects, prefer generic facial treatment while keeping the event readable.

## Optional Typography

Use only user-supplied words. Integrate type with an existing axis or quiet interval and make it share the material voice of the page. Do not manufacture titles, documentary captions, quotations, credits, dates, signatures, or place names.
